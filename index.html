The Concert

There is a reason the cello is called the instrument closest to the human voice.

Romantic Cello is an evening built entirely on that idea. Brazilian cellist Christian Grosselfinger performs the melodies people already carry with them — Nessun Dorma, Hallelujah, Now We Are Free, Con te Partirò, Einaudi’s Experience — each one sung on the solo cello.

These are songs the audience knows before the first note. What changes is hearing them this way: slower, closer, without words, in a voice that goes straight past the ears. People who have never been to a concert of this kind find themselves recognising every piece, and hearing all of them differently.

It is an intimate and deeply emotional programme, designed for rooms where people come to listen — churches and chapels, theatres, concert halls, cultural centres, elegant restaurants, weddings and private celebrations.

One cellist. Music everyone already loves. Approximately 90 minutes, adapted to the occasion.

“One of the most promising Brazilian cellists.”

The Cellist

Brazilian cellist and composer Christian Grosselfinger holds a degree in Composition from UNIRIO (Federal University of the State of Rio de Janeiro) and studied cello privately in Lisbon with Paulo Gaio Lima of the Metropolitana.

Recognised by FNAC Lisbon as “one of the most promising Brazilian cellists”, he is a recipient of the PROAC 2022 award and an official endorser of REMIC Microphones (Denmark).

He has performed with orchestras including OSBJ (Rio de Janeiro), Pro-Arte Orchestra and Darstellende Kunst Orchestra (Vienna), and Academica da Metropolitana (Lisbon), with appearances at the UNESCO Bienal Peace in Luanda and the Virada Cultural Paulista.

He plays around 36 concerts a year across Europe, touring by motorhome and booking every date himself — in theatres, churches, jazz venues and festivals.

Romantic Cello is the programme he returns to most often — the one for rooms that want to come close and stay quiet.

For Venues & Promoters

Romantic Cello is designed to be easy to host. Everything runs internally through the artist’s own system — if you have a PA and a mixer, there is almost nothing for you to prepare.

On stage
1 musician — always performed solo
Format
Solo cello
Duration
~90 minutes, adaptable
Minimum stage
3 × 2 m
Set-up / soundcheck
1 hour / 30 minutes
Venue provides
2 × XLR (stereo) · power on stage · one chair without arms

DI boxes, microphone and monitoring are all internal and carried by the artist. A 2-channel desk is sufficient.
